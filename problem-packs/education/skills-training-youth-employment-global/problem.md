# Youth Skills Training And Employment Outcome Gaps In Low-Income Countries

## Problem Statement

ILO estimates 67 million youth are unemployed globally, with youth unemployment rates 3-4 times adult rates in many low- and middle-income countries. The standard policy response — expanding vocational training and skills programs — has produced mixed results. Published evaluations report heterogeneous results that depend on program design, local labor-market conditions, employer linkage, and the outcome window. This pack does not rely on a single pooled effect estimate; it treats the gap between training enrollment and verified employment outcomes as the core evaluation problem.

Sub-national data on where training programs exist, what skills they teach, who enrolls, and whether graduates find employment is sparse and uneven across LMICs. Training-provider registries are fragmented across government ministries, NGOs, private operators, and informal apprenticeship systems. This pack does not yet have a verified cross-country denominator for the share of programs that track graduate employment; the source inventory must classify outcome-tracking evidence rather than repeat an unsupported percentage. Without verified outcome data, skills-training investment cannot be evidence-targeted.

> Which sub-national areas in LMICs have the strongest evidence-backed gap between youth skills-training availability and verified employment outcomes, and where is the data most insufficient?

## Known Facts

- Verified fact: ILO's 2024 Global Employment Trends for Youth estimates 67 million unemployed youth globally, with youth-to-adult unemployment ratios of 3-4x in many LMICs and skills mismatch identified as a primary barrier to youth employment.
- Verified fact: ILO's School-to-Work Transition Survey provides youth labour-market microdata from more than 30 countries surveyed between 2012 and 2016, with an employer labour-demand survey in seven countries, but access and country coverage are not uniform.
- Verified fact: The World Bank STEP program combines household and employer surveys on skills supply, skills demand, employer training, and labour-market outcomes, but its listed data are largely urban and were collected between 2012 and 2017.
- Verified fact: UNESCO-UNEVOC TVET country profiles provide system-level TVET information and comparative statistical context, but they are not a complete sub-national registry of government, NGO, private, or informal training providers.
- Verified fact: ILOSTAT publishes harmonized youth and informal-employment concepts from labour-force and household-survey sources, but these indicators do not identify training-provider participation or graduate-level causal outcomes.

## Uncertain Areas

- Whether any reported training effect generalizes across LMIC labour-market contexts, or whether effects are concentrated in specific sectors, provider designs, and regions.
- How informal-sector employment and self-employment interact with formal training outcomes, and whether the available surveys capture those pathways consistently.
- Whether skills-gap maps change training-provider targeting, curriculum design, or employer-engagement decisions.

## Initial Scope

This pack accepts work on:

- Evidence inventory and source-quality assessment for training-provider data, employment-outcome surveys, and labor-market information systems.
- Sub-national training-availability mapping using provider registries and program documentation.
- Employment-outcome analysis linking training data to labor-force survey and enterprise survey data.
- Red-team review of training-effectiveness overstatement and the risk of expanding programs without outcome evidence.
- Field-reality review with training providers, employer organizations, and labor-market information systems.

This pack does not accept training-program design recommendations, curriculum proposals, or unvalidated claims that specific training programs improve employment outcomes.

## Done Condition For V0

The pack is useful when it can produce one reviewed and replicated sub-national skills-training availability and employment-outcome analysis for at least two LMICs, with explicit documentation of training-provider data fragmentation, employment-outcome tracking limitations, and the distinction between training enrollment and verified employment impact.
