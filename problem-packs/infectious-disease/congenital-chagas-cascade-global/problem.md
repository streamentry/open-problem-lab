# Congenital Chagas Detection And Treatment Cascade In Endemic And Migrant-Serving Systems

## Problem Statement

The missing operational evidence is not whether congenital Chagas disease exists. It is whether health systems complete the mother-to-child prevention cascade: identifying an infected woman, confirming maternal infection, testing an exposed newborn at the correct time, treating an infected infant, and documenting follow-up. A policy, a donated medicine, or a positive maternal test is not a completed prevention outcome.

> Which maternal-newborn cascade bottlenecks are measurable and decision-relevant enough to guide screening and follow-up financing without turning modeled burden into a clinical recommendation?

## Known Facts

- Verified fact: WHO estimates about 8 million people are infected with _T. cruzi_, more than 10 000 deaths annually, and over 10 000 congenital cases annually.
- Verified fact: WHO identifies congenital transmission during pregnancy or birth as a major transmission route and reports an estimated 3–5% transmission rate among pregnancies with maternal infection.
- Verified fact: WHO identifies screening of women of childbearing age, newborn testing, and early treatment as prevention priorities. WHO's 2026 campaign states that treatment of infected newborns in the first year has a cure rate exceeding 90%.
- Verified fact: WHO's elimination roadmap includes interruption of congenital transmission and 75% antiparasitic treatment coverage of the eligible population.

## Uncertain Areas

- Country-comparable denominators for infected pregnant women, exposed newborns, confirmed infant cases, treatment initiation, treatment completion, and follow-up.
- Whether antenatal-care records, laboratory systems, birth registers, and NTD programs can be linked without double-counting or exposing personal data.
- How diagnostic timing, loss to follow-up, migration, and private-sector care change observed cascade performance.
- Whether reported program initiation represents effective coverage or only policy and procurement capacity.

## Decision Wedge

The primary decision-maker is a ministry of health or donor deciding whether to fund antenatal screening, confirmatory testing, newborn follow-up, or data-system linkage in a named country or migrant-serving network. The evidence that would change that decision is a replicated bottleneck map showing where people are lost between maternal identification and completed infant treatment, with uncertainty intervals and a non-use statement. This pack does not rank countries from infection estimates alone.

## Initial Scope

This pack accepts:

- Source inventories and measure dictionaries for each cascade stage.
- Country or network analyses that preserve numerator, denominator, test timing, and follow-up definitions.
- Linkage and missingness audits using de-identified or synthetic data.
- Red-team and field-reality review of screening, treatment, migration, stigma, and data-protection failure modes.

This pack does not accept:

- Individual diagnosis or treatment advice.
- Claims that medicine donation or national policy equals service coverage.
- Population prevalence estimates presented as observed congenital-case counts.
- Identifiable maternal, newborn, migration, or clinical records.

## V0 Done Condition

The pack is useful when one country or migrant-serving network has a reviewed and independently replicated cascade table that distinguishes maternal screening, maternal diagnosis, newborn testing, infant treatment, and follow-up, with explicit missingness and non-use boundaries.
