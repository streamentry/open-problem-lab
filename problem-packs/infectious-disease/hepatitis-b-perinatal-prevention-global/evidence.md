## Evidence Notes

The WHO fact sheet and global report establish burden and high-level service
coverage context. They do not supply a linked maternal-newborn denominator.
The WHO guideline and position paper establish the recommended pathway and the
need to distinguish timely from late birth dose, but recommendations are not
observations.

The Moturi and Papua New Guinea assessments are useful primary implementation
evidence because they expose the difference between any birth dose and a timely
birth dose, as well as data and outreach failures. Neither is nationally
representative or current enough to support a global coverage claim. A future
contributor should treat them as measurement-pattern evidence and locate a
current, representative country record before making an allocation-facing
comparison.

## Suggested Next Issue

The logical next issue is a scoped source-and-measure inventory for one named
high-burden country. It should test whether antenatal, maternity, laboratory,
and EPI systems can produce separate denominators for maternal testing,
prophylaxis, timely birth dose, infant-series completion, and impact validation.
Do not open an additional issue unless the task board requests it; the
`source-inventory` task in `tasks.json` is the entry lane.

## Evidence Quality Rule

Do not combine a WHO modeled burden estimate with a national birth count to
infer local infections. Do not combine a policy, procurement, stock, or
reported any-dose indicator with a timely birth-dose numerator. Every future
claim must state the source grain, denominator, event timing, missingness, and
what observation would make the claim fail.
