# Hepatitis B Perinatal Prevention Cascade In High-Burden Countries

## Problem Statement

The missing operational evidence is not whether hepatitis B is preventable. It
is whether a health system completes the perinatal prevention pathway: identify
maternal infection, record whether prophylaxis was eligible and started, deliver
the newborn birth dose on time, complete the infant series, and validate impact
with an appropriate outcome measure.

> Which measurable bottleneck in the hepatitis B perinatal prevention cascade
> should a ministry, funder, or immunization programme finance next, without
> treating policy, procurement, stock, any-dose coverage, or a modeled burden
> estimate as delivered protection?

## Decision Wedge

The primary decision users are a ministry of health or national immunization
technical advisory group choosing among timely birth-dose delivery, maternal
HBsAg testing and antiviral prophylaxis, outreach for births outside facilities,
and linkage between antenatal, maternity, laboratory, and EPI records. A global
funder may face the same choice when deciding whether a country needs commodity
finance, service-delivery support, or measurement-system support.

The evidence that would change the decision is a reviewed and replicated table
for a named country or health-system network that keeps these denominators
separate:

1. pregnancies or antenatal attendees eligible for HBsAg screening;
2. screened pregnancies with a recorded result;
3. eligible pregnancies with a recorded prophylaxis event;
4. live births with a recorded birth-dose administration time;
5. infants with subsequent scheduled doses recorded; and
6. a separately defined serological or infection outcome used only for impact
   validation.

The output must expose missingness, private-sector and home-birth coverage,
linkage failure, reporting lag, and uncertainty. It must not produce a clinical
recommendation or a league table.

## Strongest Objection And Response

**Objection:** HBV already has WHO guidance, a vaccine, global targets, and
immunization reporting. Creating another pack could duplicate a well-served
field rather than redirect effort.

**Response:** The objection is valid about policy attention but not about
decision-grade implementation evidence. WHO reports timely birth-dose coverage
of only 45% globally and below 20% in the African Region, while the 2024 global
report combines burden and service-coverage information rather than an
end-to-end linked maternal-newborn cascade. The narrow contribution here is to
make the implementation gap falsifiable: distinguish a late dose from a timely
dose, administration from stock, and maternal eligibility from prophylaxis
initiation. If a country already has those linked measurements, this pack should
document that negative result and move on.

## Known Facts

- **Verified fact:** WHO estimates that 240 million people were living with
  chronic hepatitis B in 2024, with about 0.9 million new infections each year
  and 1.1 million deaths in 2024. WHO also states that infection in infancy or
  early childhood becomes chronic in about 95% of cases. See the current [WHO
  hepatitis B fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/hepatitis-b).
- **Verified fact:** WHO reported in March 2024 that hepatitis B birth-dose
  coverage was 45% globally and below 20% in the WHO African Region. See [WHO
  publishes new guidelines on hepatitis B](https://www.who.int/news/item/29-03-2024-who-publishes-updated-guidelines-on-hepatitis-b).
- **Verified fact:** WHO recommends a birth dose for all infants as soon as
  possible after birth, ideally within 24 hours, and recommends reporting late
  birth doses separately from timely doses. See the [WHO hepatitis B vaccine
  position paper](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WER9227).
- **Verified fact:** WHO's 2024 chronic-HBV guideline expands eligibility for
  antiviral prophylaxis among pregnant women and updates testing and service
  delivery recommendations. A guideline establishes an intervention pathway;
  it does not establish that a country delivered it.
- **Verified fact:** A five-country African facility assessment found that
  total birth-dose coverage could be substantially higher than timely coverage,
  and that reporting tools often failed to record timing, while home-birth
  outreach and written operating procedures were absent in the assessed
  facilities. See [Moturi et al., 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6436721/).

## Uncertain Areas

- Whether reported national birth-dose indicators use the same denominator and
  distinguish administration within 24 hours from a later dose.
- How many high-burden countries can link maternal HBsAg results, prophylaxis
  events, birth time, dose time, and later infant records without double-counting
  or exposing personal data.
- How home births, private maternity care, migration, stillbirth definitions,
  and delayed registration alter the observed cascade.
- Whether a recorded vaccine dose was administered, administered on time, or
  merely procured, stocked, or entered into a summary report.
- Which impact measures are feasible and interpretable: childhood HBsAg
  prevalence, infant infection outcomes, or another validated serological
  measure. Impact must not be inferred from service inputs alone.

## Initial Scope

This pack accepts:

- source inventories and measurement dictionaries for each cascade stage;
- country or health-system analyses that preserve event grain, timing, and
  denominator definitions;
- de-identified or synthetic linkage and missingness audits;
- reviewed comparisons of delivery pathways for facility and home births;
- red-team and field-reality review of stigma, coercion, privacy, and clinical
  overreach risks.

This pack does not accept:

- individual diagnosis, vaccination, or antiviral-treatment advice;
- a country or facility ranking based only on modeled burden or reported
  coverage;
- claims that policy adoption, procurement, stock, or dose availability equals
  administration or protection;
- a claim that maternal HBsAg status alone predicts an infant outcome without
  appropriate diagnostic and follow-up evidence;
- identifiable maternal, newborn, household, migration, or clinical records.

## V0 Done Condition

The pack is useful when one named country or health-system network has a
reviewed and independently replicated cascade table that distinguishes maternal
screening, prophylaxis, timely birth dose, infant-series completion, and impact
validation, with explicit missingness, privacy, and non-use boundaries.
