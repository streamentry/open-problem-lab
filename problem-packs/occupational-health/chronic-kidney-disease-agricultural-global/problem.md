# Chronic Kidney Disease Of Uncertain Etiology In Agricultural Communities

## Problem Statement

Chronic kidney disease of uncertain etiology (CKDu) — also described in parts of the literature as CKD of unknown etiology, CKD of nontraditional causes, or chronic interstitial nephritis in agricultural communities — has been reported in agricultural and other manual-worker populations in Central America, South Asia, Sri Lanka, and other tropical or subtropical settings. The labels are not automatically interchangeable, and a low eGFR observed once is not enough to establish persistent CKD.

The v0 problem is not “find the cause of CKDu” or “ban a suspected exposure.” Those framings convert heterogeneous evidence into premature action. The sharper problem is:

> Which agricultural and manual-worker populations have reproducible evidence of persistent CKDu/CINAC after separating transient acute kidney injury, traditional CKD, and uncertain exposure reports, and would a standardized surveillance result change a named health or labor decision?

## Decision Wedge

The primary decision users are:

1. a Ministry of Health NCD or occupational-health director deciding whether to fund sentinel kidney surveillance and primary-care case finding;
2. a labor ministry or occupational-health authority deciding whether a worker-protection investigation is justified; and
3. a health or research funder deciding whether to support repeated kidney-function measurement, exposure assessment, or a cross-country surveillance platform.

The evidence that would change the decision is a reviewed and independently replicated setting-level matrix that preserves:

1. persistent CKD confirmation, including repeat testing at least 90 days apart;
2. the operational CKDu/CINAC definition and its traditional-risk-factor exclusions;
3. kidney phenotype fields such as eGFR, albuminuria or proteinuria, and markers of tubular injury;
4. agricultural or manual-work population, age, sex, employment, and sampling frame;
5. objective versus self-reported heat, hydration, water, agrochemical, medication, and infection exposures;
6. transient AKI, missingness, referral, and loss-to-follow-up handling; and
7. the specific decision the evidence can inform without claiming causality.

## Strongest Objection And Response

**Objection:** CKD already has a global policy agenda, and CKDu has been studied for more than two decades. A new pack could duplicate nephrology research or amplify a contested causal story.

**Response:** The objection is valid about existing attention and is a reason to keep this pack narrow. The proposed contribution is not a new etiologic theory or a new global prevalence estimate. The 2026 ISN i3C report states that CKDu burden differences are poorly described, terminology is inconsistent, disease onset is difficult to distinguish from progression, and exposure capture needs alternatives to self-report. A measurement pack can therefore add value even where research exists: it can make studies comparable enough to decide whether a setting warrants surveillance or a causal investigation. If harmonization shows that existing country data already support a comparable estimate, the pack should record that negative result and move to replication rather than manufacture another index.

## Known Facts

- **Verified fact:** WHO estimates that 674 million people have chronic kidney disease worldwide, most in low- and middle-income countries. WHO also states that persistent eGFR below 60 or persistent albuminuria is used to identify CKD and that simple blood and urine tests can detect it ([WHO kidney disease](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/kidney-disease)).
- **Verified fact:** A 2025 population-based Tamil Nadu study screened 3,350 agricultural workers, estimated CKD prevalence at 5.31% and CKDu prevalence at 2.66%, and found that 406 participants with a low first eGFR had a normal result at repeat testing, illustrating why transient kidney injury and persistent CKD must be separated ([CKDu-AGRI Study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lansea.2025.100683)).
- **Verified fact:** A 2022 Sri Lankan rural study reported suspected CKDu in 8.5% of 352 participants and 63.8% of observed CKD cases, but used a selected local sample and cannot establish national or global prevalence ([Wijewickrama et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ekir.2022.07.012)).
- **Verified fact:** The MANOS prospective cohort used repeated serum testing and worksite observation among workers in El Salvador and Nicaragua; baseline CKD prevalence was 7.4% overall and age-standardized prevalence was 14.1% among Salvadoran sugarcane workers ([Keogh et al.](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12882-022-02861-0)).
- **Verified fact:** The ISN i3C report identifies poor description of cross-region CKDu burden, inconsistent terminology, difficulty distinguishing disease onset from progression, and the need for alternatives to self-reported exposures as current field obstacles ([ISN i3C report](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10220673)).
- **Verified fact:** A Sri Lankan paper provides a multilevel clinical case-definition framework for CKDu surveillance and epidemiological studies, showing that case-definition design is an actionable measurement object even though the framework is region-specific ([Wijewickrama et al. case definition](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ekir.2019.03.020)).

## Uncertain Areas

- The global number of people with CKDu is unknown. Total CKD burden must not be used as a CKDu denominator.
- CKDu, CKD of nontraditional causes, CINAC, Mesoamerican nephropathy, and regional names may overlap clinically without being definitionally identical.
- Associations with heat, dehydration, agrochemicals, water quality, heavy metals, infection, NSAIDs, or nutrition do not establish a single cause or a transferable intervention.
- A low eGFR during heat exposure may represent transient AKI, early CKD, measurement error, or selection effects; repeat timing and assay calibration matter.
- Primary studies may omit informal workers, women, adolescents, non-agricultural manual workers, people who do not attend facilities, and people who leave employment after illness.
- A country-level surveillance result may not justify a workplace standard, clinical screening policy, water intervention, or pesticide restriction without causal and field review.

## Initial Scope

This pack accepts:

- Evidence inventories for CKDu/CINAC case definitions, repeated kidney-function measurement, and exposure assessment.
- Cross-study measurement dictionaries preserving eGFR method, repeat timing, albuminuria/proteinuria, exclusion criteria, phenotype, and denominator.
- Country or region source audits using de-identified or synthetic data with explicit missingness and privacy boundaries.
- Comparisons of objective exposure measures, worksite observation, biological samples, environmental samples, and self-report without treating them as interchangeable.
- Red-team review of causal overreach, worker blame, employer-liability misuse, unsupported exposure bans, and inappropriate clinical interpretation.
- Field-reality review with ministries of health, labor authorities, occupational-health services, nephrology teams, and worker organizations.

This pack does not accept:

- Individual diagnosis or medical advice.
- Claims that one pesticide, contaminant, heat metric, or water source causes CKDu without replicated causal evidence.
- Pesticide bans, water restrictions, work-rest mandates, employment exclusion, or clinical treatment instructions.
- Country or worker rankings before domain review, red-team review, field-reality review, and independent replication.
- Identifiable health, employment, precise workplace, or vulnerable-community data.

## Done Condition For V0

The pack is useful when it produces one reviewed and independently replicated cross-setting surveillance matrix covering at least two agricultural or manual-worker settings, with persistent-CKD confirmation, operational CKDu definitions, traditional-risk-factor exclusions, exposure measurement mode, missingness, and a named non-clinical decision the matrix can inform without issuing a causal or allocation order.
