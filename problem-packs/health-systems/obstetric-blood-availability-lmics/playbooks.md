# Playbooks

## Analyst Playbook

1. Start with `problem.json`, `evidence.json`, `datasets.md`, and `tasks.json`.
2. Define the facility, blood product, observation window, and obstetric episode before calculating a rate.
3. Keep national donation, facility readiness, stock interruption, referral delay, compatibility, issue, and transfusion as separate variables.
4. Record missingness and reporting lag before comparing facilities or countries.
5. Do not infer clinical safety or maternal benefit from supply indicators alone.

## Reviewer Playbook

Ask five questions:

1. What exact blood-pathway claim is being made?
2. Does the source measure national supply, facility readiness, interruption, compatibility, referral, issue, or transfusion?
3. What is the observation window and what is missing?
4. What would make the comparison false or misleading?
5. Who could misuse the output, and what gate blocks that use?

## Field-Reality Playbook

Ask whether the output changes a named decision for a blood-service director, maternal-health director, hospital transfusion committee, or referral-network manager at a useful time. A map that arrives after a stockout is not an early-warning tool. A readiness score that cannot distinguish survey-day presence from emergency reliability is not a time-to-transfusion measure.
