# Obstetric Blood Availability And Time-To-Transfusion Gaps In LMICs

## Problem Statement

Postpartum haemorrhage is preventable and treatable, but a national blood-donation rate is not the same thing as a woman being able to receive safe, compatible blood at a facility during a haemorrhage. The unresolved operational question is:

> At which step, and at which facility, does the blood pathway fail before a patient with obstetric haemorrhage can receive a safe transfusion within the relevant emergency window?

This pack is about measurement and verification of that pathway. It is not a clinical protocol, a transfusion threshold, or a model that directs patient care.

## Known Facts

- Verified fact: WHO reports that postpartum haemorrhage affects about 14 million women annually and causes around 70,000 maternal deaths each year.
- Verified fact: WHO's 2026 blood-safety summary reports a median of 4.5 donations per 1,000 people in low-income countries, compared with 28.9 in high-income countries, and states that many patients requiring transfusion do not have timely access to safe blood.
- Verified fact: WHO's facility-assessment instruments include observable blood-transfusion readiness indicators and questions about interruptions in blood availability during the previous three months.
- Verified fact: WHO's African Region reported in 2021 that roughly half of the blood needed for transfusion in the region was collected, while noting the particular importance of blood for obstetric haemorrhage.

## Uncertain Areas

- Whether national donation and blood-safety statistics predict facility-level stock interruptions or time-to-transfusion.
- Whether facility surveys capture emergency availability, compatibility testing, transport, night/weekend staffing, and referral delay with enough precision.
- How often reported readiness on the day of a survey differs from actual availability during an obstetric emergency.
- Which combination of blood-service, facility, referral, and maternal-health records can be linked without exposing personal health data.

## Initial Scope

This pack accepts:

- Evidence inventories for blood-supply, facility-readiness, obstetric-care, referral, and stock-interruption measures.
- Indicator crosswalks that preserve definition, numerator, denominator, grain, and time reference.
- Facility-level data-quality assessments and missingness audits.
- Reproducible analyses of availability, interruption, referral delay, and time-to-transfusion where de-identified data are lawfully available.
- Red-team and field-reality review of resource-allocation interpretations.

This pack does not accept individual clinical advice, transfusion thresholds, patient-level prediction, public facility rankings without validation, or resource-allocation recommendations based only on national donation rates.

## Done Condition For V0

The pack is useful when a reviewer can trace at least one facility-level measurement pathway from blood collection or stock records to obstetric emergency care, identify its missingness and grain limitations, and state which named health-system decision it could inform without treating readiness as proof of clinical outcomes.
