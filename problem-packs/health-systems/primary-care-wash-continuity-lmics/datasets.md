## Required Dataset Properties

Any dataset used in this pack must document:

- facility identifier, facility type, managing authority, rurality, service area, and the sampling frame or census rule;
- observation date, season, source and storage type, pump or pipe state, water availability when needed, quantity proxy, and any water-quality sample or treatment record;
- named points of care and toilets, station function, water, soap or alcohol-based hand rub, hand-drying, accessibility, and observation mode;
- stock-out, outage, repair, replenishment, and unresolved states with a defined start and end event;
- whether each value is observed, logged, self-reported, sampled, imputed, refused, not observed, or not applicable;
- missingness, non-response, excluded facilities, private or faith-based coverage, conflict or mobile-service coverage, and linkage failures; and
- data owner, permission, retention, access control, de-identification, and whether the artifact can be independently rerun.

## Harmonization Rules

1. Do not combine the JMP no-service estimate with the JMP basic-service estimate as though they are the same year or denominator.
2. Do not map an improved source or on-premises source directly to continuous, sufficient, or safe water.
3. Do not treat a facility audit as a population access estimate or a patient-level outcome.
4. Do not combine water, hand hygiene, sanitation, environmental cleaning, waste, electricity, IPC, and clinical outcomes into a composite score without a field-level measurement dictionary.
5. When instruments differ, retain source-specific values and record the incompatibility; do not harmonize by prose or unreported assumptions.

## Key Gap

The reviewed sources provide strong definitions, facility-audit methods, repeated improvement tools, and several historical examples of continuity and water-quality measurement. They do not yet provide a current, open, comparable, population-weighted dataset that links primary-care facility water and hand-hygiene availability when needed to quantity, quality, point-of-care function, stock-out or repair intervals, facility frame, and missingness across LMICs.

That gap is an empirical question, not a licence to assume absence. The next country-bounded task should search authorized Ministry of Health, HHFA, SARA, SPA, WASH FIT, water-quality, and maintenance records and may conclude that a compatible dataset already exists.
