# Evidence Ledger

The machine-readable ledger is `evidence.json`.

## Evidence Notes

### WHO global burden and indicator

Use the WHO fact sheet for burden and regional framing only. Use the GHO metadata for the exact reliability definition, denominator behavior, source families, and known variation in outage windows. Neither source is a facility uptime panel or a patient-outcome study.

### WHO assessment instruments

The HHFA combined core questionnaire is the strongest starting instrument for distinguishing electricity function in a service area from backup availability. It is an instrument, not evidence that every country has collected the fields or that the fields are comparable after local adaptation.

### Malawi facility studies

Reuland et al. provide detailed evidence that power interruptions, backup failure, and service conditions can be observed together in a small mixed-methods facility sample. Ngongoma et al. show a more recent way to connect power categories to a named device while preserving the denominator for equipment functionality. Both are setting-specific and observational.

### Ghana and Uganda implementation study

Javadi et al. demonstrate that electrification can be assessed with a staged implementation design and mixed methods. The study is useful for tractability and for separating readiness from service use: improved readiness does not automatically imply increased utilization or a clinical outcome.

## Evidence Quality Rule

No source is treated as proof of a facility ranking, procurement return, clinical quality, or mortality effect unless it measures that outcome with an appropriate design. Connection, current function, reliability, backup, load capacity, device function, service availability, service use, and patient outcome remain separate evidence fields.
