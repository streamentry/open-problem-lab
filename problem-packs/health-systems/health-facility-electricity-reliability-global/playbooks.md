# Playbooks

## Analyst Playbook

1. Start with `problem.json`, `evidence.json`, and `tasks.json`.
2. Separate electricity presence, current function, reliability, backup, capacity, service-area reach, maintenance, device function, service availability, service use, and patient outcome.
3. Preserve facility level, ownership, geography, sampling frame, observation window, measurement mode, season, missingness, and weights.
4. Add evidence and a measure crosswalk before adding a quantitative output.
5. Treat any financing, procurement, clinical, or facility-ranking implication as blocked until the required reviewers and independent replication are recorded.

## Reviewer Playbook

Ask:

1. What is the denominator: facilities, service areas, devices, beds, hours, or patients?
2. Was electricity observed, self-reported, logged, sensed, or inferred from a connection or installed device?
3. What is the outage threshold and look-back window, and does it match the named service's operating hours?
4. Does the backup source function, have fuel or battery capacity, and power the required load?
5. Could the result be an artifact of facility level, ownership, selection, season, missingness, maintenance, or donor targeting?
6. Who could use the result to buy equipment that cannot be powered, maintained, or safely used?

## Field-Reality Playbook

The named decision-maker must be a ministry health-infrastructure lead, biomedical-engineering unit, rural electrification agency, facility network manager, or funder portfolio manager. The named decision must be a measurable choice about assessment, repair, maintenance, backup, load capacity, or monitoring. A gap map alone is not a recommendation, and a facility with missing data must not be treated as a failed facility.
