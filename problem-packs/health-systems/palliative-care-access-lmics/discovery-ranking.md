# Discovery Ranking

## Selection Method

I screened uncovered problem areas against four tests: quantified burden, a plausible measurement or intervention path, evidence of under-service or under-measurement, and a named decision that verified evidence could change. The ranking is a strategic screen, not a claim that the candidates are directly comparable. Existing packs were checked across 16 live domains and 118 packs on 31 August 2026.

## Selection Decision

Palliative-care access and continuity is the strongest uncovered candidate because the gap is not merely that services are scarce: the available global statistics still do not tell a named health-system decision-maker where need becomes unreachable, unserved, unsupported, unaffordable, or lost. WHO has supplied a feasible indicator architecture, while the repo has no pack dedicated to this cascade. The first task is deliberately a source inventory; no global ranking or clinical recommendation is asserted.

## Candidate Notes

Palliative-care access has a high-severity, measurable burden and a direct allocation wedge. WHO estimates 56.8 million people need palliative care annually, including 25.7 million in the last year of life, while its 2020 fact sheet reports roughly 14% receipt and only 40% of 194 Member States reporting that services reached at least half of patients in need. WHO's 2021 indicator framework and 2018 primary-care guide give a plausible measurement and service path. The unresolved question is whether a country can link need, referral, actual care, medicine access, caregiver support, and continuity well enough for a Ministry of Health or funder to change a budget decision. Sources: [WHO fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/palliative-care), [WHO indicators](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240033351), and [WHO primary-care guide](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/integrating-palliative-care-and-symptom-relief-into-primary-health-care).

Falls are a serious alternative: WHO estimates 684,000 fatal falls and 37.3 million medically attended falls per year, with over 80% of fatalities in LMICs. The prevention path is credible because WHO's Step Safely package identifies strategies for community-dwelling and institutionalized older people. It ranked below palliative care because a first pack would span injury prevention, fracture care, rehabilitation, and older-person services that already have neighboring portfolio coverage, making the first allocation decision less concentrated. Sources: [WHO falls fact sheet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/falls) and [Step Safely](https://www.who.int/teams/social-determinants-of-health/safety-and-mobility/step-safely).

Appropriate wheelchair service coverage is another strong candidate. WHO estimates that about 80 million people, or 1% of the world's population, are likely to require a wheelchair, while its access page says access to assistive products can be as low as 3% in some settings. WHO supplies wheelchair-provision guidance and rATA, a household tool measuring need, access, barriers, and satisfaction. It ranked below palliative care because the broader assistive-technology estimate covers heterogeneous products and the repository already has disability, hearing, and blindness access packs; a new wheelchair pack would need a carefully bounded ownership case. Sources: [WHO wheelchair guidance release](https://www.who.int/news/item/05-06-2023-who-releases-new-wheelchair-provision-guidelines), [WHO assistive-technology access](https://www.who.int/teams/health-product-and-policy-standards/assistive-and-medical-technology/assistive-technology/promoting-access), and [rATA](https://www.who.int/tools/ata-toolkit/rata).

Neonatal serious bacterial infection diagnosis and treatment is high-stakes and tractable. WHO's 2025 target-product profile reports 2.3 million newborn deaths annually, says sepsis contributes 15%, and identifies inadequate or missing diagnostics in many settings. The candidate ranked below palliative care because neonatal mortality, maternal-child health, and diagnostic-development institutions already provide strong evidence pipelines, and the repository has a neonatal mortality and quality-of-care pack. Its immediate clinical-treatment implications also demand a narrower operational scope before discovery. Source: [WHO target product profile](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240113015).

Affordable low-emissions cooling is large and actionable. IEA reports 3.5 billion people live in hot climates, only about 15% owned air conditioning in 2021, and heat-related deaths among people aged 65 and over reached an estimated 300,000 or more per year in 2019–2021. It ranked below palliative care because the repository already covers urban heat, heat early warning, clean cooking, and energy access; the bottleneck is likely coordination across those packs rather than a missing problem definition. Source: [IEA sustainable affordable cooling analysis](https://www.iea.org/reports/sustainable-affordable-cooling-can-save-tens-of-thousands-of-lives-each-year).
