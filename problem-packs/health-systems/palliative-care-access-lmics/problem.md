# Palliative Care Access And Continuity Gaps In Low- And Middle-Income Countries

## Problem Statement

Palliative care is often measured as a policy, a hospice count, or an opioid-consumption statistic. None of those alone answers the question a health-system decision-maker must answer: which people with serious health-related suffering can obtain acceptable, person-centred support, where does the pathway fail, and what allocation would close the measured gap?

The sharper problem is:

> Can a low- or middle-income country measure a reproducible palliative-care cascade from need to referral, first contact, continuing symptom support, caregiver support, essential-medicine access, transfer, and documented outcome, at a grain that can change a real health-system allocation?

This pack treats palliative care as integrated support for people and families facing problems associated with life-threatening illness. It includes physical, psychosocial, social, and spiritual needs, but it does not assume that a diagnosis, a death record, a morphine statistic, or a facility's self-description proves that care was received.

## Decision Wedge

The first decision-maker is a named Ministry of Health UHC, primary-care, or NCD director, working with the national medicines regulator and a health funder. The decision is whether to finance or revise a bounded package of primary/community/home-based palliative care, referral capacity, workforce training, caregiver support, and essential medicines in a defined country or district.

Evidence changes that decision only if it can distinguish:

- people who meet a stated need definition from people who happen to appear in a facility or mortality dataset
- a service listed in policy from a service that is staffed, reachable, open, and able to accept a referral
- first contact from continuing symptom support and documented follow-up
- medicine authorization, availability, affordability, dispensing, and appropriate use
- patient-defined goals and caregiver support from provider-reported activity
- a feasible allocation target from a national average that hides district or socioeconomic inequity

The first contribution is therefore a measurement and source crosswalk, not a clinical protocol or a claim that any service model reduces mortality or hospitalization.

## Known Facts

- **Verified fact:** WHO's 2020 palliative-care fact sheet estimates that 56.8 million people need palliative care each year, including 25.7 million in the last year of life, and reports that about 14% of people who need it receive it (evidence: `who-palliative-care-burden-access-2020`).
- **Verified fact:** The same WHO fact sheet reports that, in a 2019 survey of 194 Member States, funding for palliative care was available in 68% of countries and only 40% reported that services reached at least half of patients in need (evidence: `who-palliative-care-burden-access-2020`). This is a country-reported capacity signal, not a patient-level coverage estimate.
- **Verified fact:** WHO's 2021 actionable-indicator report defines a country- and subnationally adaptable measurement set covering policy, service provision, essential medicines, education and training, people and communities, and research (evidence: `who-palliative-care-actionable-indicators-2021`).
- **Verified fact:** WHO's primary-health-care guide frames palliative care as a community and primary-care function, with referral to higher levels when needed, and emphasizes continuity, respect for patient values, equity, and attention to families (evidence: `who-primary-care-palliative-guide-2018`).
- **Verified fact:** WHO's 2023 morphine-access report identifies morphine as an essential medicine for moderate-to-severe pain and refractory breathlessness, including in palliative care, and examines barriers to safe medical access (evidence: `who-morphine-access-2023`).
- **Verified fact:** INCB's 2019 technical publication reports that in 2018 the 79% of the world's population living mainly in low- and middle-income countries consumed 13% of morphine used for pain and suffering; the statistic describes distribution of consumption, not palliative-care coverage or appropriate prescribing (evidence: `incb-narcotic-drugs-2019`).
- **Verified fact:** WHO's Harmonized Health Facility Assessment can measure facility service availability, readiness, quality, management, and finance using facility visits, observation, interviews, and records, but it does not by itself establish population need, patient preference, continuity, or clinical benefit (evidence: `who-hhfa-introduction-2024`).

## Uncertain Areas

- How the 56.8-million global need estimate behaves when applied to a named LMIC, age group, disease mix, or district, and whether the numerator is comparable with a local serious-health-related-suffering or end-of-life definition.
- How many people are referred, reached, and followed over time rather than merely counted as a policy target, a registered service, a facility contact, or a death.
- Whether primary-care and home/community services have trained staff, supervision, referral fallbacks, essential medicines, caregiver support, and a way to record patient-defined goals and complaints.
- Whether medicine scarcity is caused by procurement, affordability, distribution, prescriber training, regulation, fear of diversion, or an interaction among these factors; morphine consumption alone cannot identify the bottleneck.
- How children, older people, people with HIV or drug-resistant tuberculosis, people with non-cancer conditions, people in rural or conflict-affected areas, and people outside formal facilities are represented in existing data.
- Whether a harmonized source and indicator set would change an actual benefits-package, workforce, service-location, or procurement decision after accounting for cost, data burden, privacy, and local preferences.

## Initial Scope

This pack accepts:

- dated primary and official sources for need estimation, service mapping, referral, medicines, workforce, caregiver support, and patient or family outcomes
- field-level crosswalks that preserve adult/child, disease, geography, facility, community, home, and socioeconomic strata
- explicit separation of policy, financing, service availability, readiness, contact, continuity, medicine access, and outcome measures
- denominator, numerator, missingness, non-response, referral, attrition, and privacy documentation
- source inventories and negative results showing that a proposed cross-country comparison is not valid
- a reviewed, country-bounded measurement protocol that names a decision-maker, allocation decision, data owner, and non-use case
- red-team review of opioid, clinical, privacy, safeguarding, stigma, and false-reassurance risks

This pack does not accept:

- diagnosis, prescribing, dose selection, or treatment advice for an individual
- a claim that morphine consumption is equivalent to palliative-care coverage, quality, or patient benefit
- a mortality, hospitalization, symptom-relief, caregiver, or cost-effect claim from a policy, facility, service-count, or cross-sectional source alone
- a global ranking of countries, providers, clinicians, or families without a compatible denominator and replicated method
- an assumption that hospice, cancer care, end-of-life care, or opioid access represents all palliative-care need
- an operational service or automated triage system before consent, clinical boundaries, referral ownership, complaints, safeguarding, and exit conditions are reviewed
- a recommendation to relax controlled-medicine safeguards; safe access and diversion prevention must be assessed together by qualified reviewers

## Done Condition For V0

V0 is complete when one geographically bounded LMIC measurement protocol has:

1. a reproducible, explicitly bounded need denominator
2. a service and referral map that distinguishes availability, readiness, reach, continuity, and transfer
3. a separate medicine-access crosswalk for authorization, procurement, availability, affordability, dispensing, and consumption
4. patient and caregiver consent, preference, outcome, complaint, equity, and missingness fields
5. a named health-system allocation decision with a pre-specified evidence threshold
6. independent domain, red-team, field-reality, and replication review before field-facing use

V0 is a measurement and decision-support protocol. It is not proof that any palliative-care model improves survival, eliminates suffering, or should be scaled nationally.
