# Playbooks

## Measurement-First Sequence

1. Name the decision-maker and decision before collecting data.
2. Lock the fall definition, age boundary, setting, recurrence window, injury threshold, and population frame.
3. Inventory population, facility, transport, rehabilitation, intervention, caregiver, and mortality sources.
4. Map fields to separate event, injury, delay, service, prevention, and function families.
5. Verify one bounded country or district pathway before proposing a cross-country comparison.
6. Replicate calculations from locked inputs and preserve divergences.
7. Complete injury/healthy-ageing domain, red-team, field-reality, and replication review before operational or allocation-facing output.

## Fall-To-Function States

```text
Fall-event -> Care-seeking-decision -> Transport -> First-facility-assessment
First-facility-assessment -> Treated -> Admitted -> Transferred -> Rehabilitation
Rehabilitation -> Functional-follow-up -> Closed
Any state -> Refused-or-unreachable
Any state -> Unresolved-or-lost-to-follow-up
```

These are data states, not clinical instructions. A fall is not necessarily an injury; a facility contact is not a completed care pathway; a rehabilitation referral is not functional recovery.

## Prevention Boundary

No individual risk score, home assessment, medication review, exercise programme, or environmental modification should be deployed from this pack alone. A responsible institution must first confirm consent, safeguarding, clinical ownership, referral, equity, complaint handling, data governance, adverse-event monitoring, and a safe exit or handover.

## Cheapest Disconfirming Tests

Stop or redesign before broader implementation if:

- no compatible population fall denominator can be constructed
- facility records cannot be linked to a named catchment or the linkage would double count cases
- time-to-care intervals do not have observable start and end events
- prevention exposure or fidelity cannot be measured separately from outcomes
- functional follow-up is missing or selectively absent for poorer, rural, disabled, or oldest participants
- the proposed intervention cannot be delivered by an identified and supervised service
- older adults or caregivers do not freely consent to the proposed data collection or home activity
- the named decision-maker cannot identify an allocation the evidence would change

Thresholds must be preregistered with local domain and field reviewers. This pack does not invent universal acceptable fall, delay, injury, adherence, or outcome thresholds.

## Non-Obvious Second-Order Risks

- A fall-risk score may redirect scarce care toward people who are easier to measure rather than those with the highest unmet need.
- Home-safety or medication programmes may shift responsibility onto older adults or caregivers for hazards created by poverty, housing, or inaccessible services.
- Improving facility registry completeness may appear to increase falls or severe injuries and be misread as programme failure.
- Digital reminders can create false reassurance while leaving transport, treatment, and rehabilitation capacity unchanged.
- Trial replication in a better-resourced district may overstate transportability to remote or conflict-affected communities.
- Prevention targets may reward reported attendance while hiding adverse effects, coercion, or people who withdrew from follow-up.
