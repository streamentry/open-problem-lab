# Older-Adult Fall Injury Prevention And Post-Fall Function In Low- And Middle-Income Countries

## Problem Statement

Falls are often treated as a single count: a mortality estimate, an emergency-department presentation, or a self-reported event. Those measures answer different questions. A prevention programme can reduce reported falls without proving fewer serious injuries; a hospital registry can show severe cases without measuring people who never reach care; a home modification can change mobility without proving a population-level fall reduction.

The sharper problem is:

> Can a low- or middle-income country measure the older-adult fall pathway from event to injury, time-to-care, treatment, rehabilitation, functional recovery, and prevention exposure well enough to decide where to allocate scarce injury and healthy-ageing resources?

This pack focuses on adults aged 60 years and older where the source supports that age band. Studies covering adults aged 50 years and older or all ages remain useful only when their age strata and case definitions are preserved. The pack treats fall prevention and post-fall care as linked but distinct questions: preventing an event is not the same as reducing disability after an event.

## Decision Wedge

The first decision-maker is a Ministry of Health healthy-ageing, injury-prevention, or primary-care director, working with a district health authority and a rehabilitation or emergency-care funder. The decision is whether to finance a bounded package of fall surveillance, community prevention, safer-home work, transport/referral improvement, fracture or trauma capacity, and rehabilitation follow-up in a named country or district.

Evidence changes that decision only if it distinguishes:

- population fall incidence and recurrence from facility presentation
- fall occurrence from injury severity and functional consequence
- home, work, road, recreational, and institutional settings
- self-report, caregiver report, registry abstraction, clinical assessment, and death registration
- time from event to decision to seek care, transport, arrival, treatment, transfer, and rehabilitation
- prevention exposure and fidelity from the outcome it is intended to change
- short-term discharge status from patient-defined functional recovery
- correlation or risk-factor association from a replicated causal intervention estimate

The first contribution is therefore a measurement and source crosswalk, not an individual risk score, clinical protocol, or universal exercise prescription.

## Known Facts

- **Verified fact:** WHO estimates 684,000 fatal falls and 37.3 million falls severe enough to require medical attention each year. More than 80% of fall-related fatalities occur in low- and middle-income countries, and death rates are highest among adults over 60 (evidence: `who-falls-global-burden-2021`).
- **Verified fact:** WHO's Step Safely package treats falls as a life-course problem and identifies evidence-informed strategies for children, workers, community-dwelling older people, older people in residential care, and older people receiving hospital care (evidence: `who-step-safely-2021`).
- **Verified fact:** A nationally representative WHO SAGE Wave 1 analysis in China, Ghana, India, Mexico, the Russian Federation, and South Africa studied adults aged 50 years and older. The pooled prevalence of self-reported past-year fall-related injury was 4.0%, and the study reported an association between fall-related injury and disability (evidence: `sage-fall-injury-lmic-2015`).
- **Verified fact:** A prospective registry study across 23 facilities in Malawi and Tanzania recorded 93,178 trauma patients, of whom 44,609 had fall-related complaints; the study reported a median 10-hour arrival interval and 3,275 potentially serious injuries, but its facilities and registry periods are not a population denominator (evidence: `sawe-fall-registry-malawi-tanzania-2023`).
- **Verified fact:** The rural-China FAMILY cluster randomized trial recruited 2,616 participants from 128 villages, randomized 2,610 adults aged 60 years or older, and compared community-engaged balance/functional exercise and health education with usual care. During follow-up, 29.7% of intervention participants and 38.3% of control participants reported at least one fall; the reported outcome was self-reported and the trial was conducted in a specific rural Chinese primary-care system (evidence: `family-fall-prevention-rct-china-2025`).
- **Verified fact:** WHO's older-adult falls report states that effective allocation requires better knowledge of scale and nature and evidence on intervention effectiveness, and gives a 28–35% annual fall estimate for people aged 65 years and over, rising to 32–42% among people over 70 (evidence: `who-older-adult-falls-report-2008`).

## Uncertain Areas

- The current population incidence and recurrence of falls among adults aged 60 years and older in most LMIC settings, including people who do not reach a facility.
- How mortality, household surveys, emergency registries, trauma registries, rehabilitation records, and community health-worker records can be linked without treating one source as a complete denominator.
- Whether time-to-care is measured from injury to care-seeking, departure, transport arrival, facility arrival, or treatment; these intervals have different intervention implications.
- Which fall mechanisms, housing and public-space hazards, medication exposures, visual or cognitive limitations, and social conditions are causal, merely associated, or confounded in a named setting.
- Whether primary-care prevention models such as the FAMILY trial transport to other LMIC health systems, age structures, languages, workforce constraints, or rural and urban environments.
- Whether functional outcomes, rehabilitation access, caregiver burden, and patient-defined goals are recorded after discharge, and whether missing follow-up is concentrated among poorer, rural, disabled, or very old participants.
- Whether a verified local cascade would change a real health, rehabilitation, transport, housing, or prevention allocation rather than simply produce another burden map.

## Initial Scope

This pack accepts:

- dated population, mortality, household, facility, injury-registry, transport, rehabilitation, and intervention sources
- crosswalks that preserve age, sex, geography, setting, fall definition, injury severity, recurrence, and case-finding mode
- measurement of transport and time-to-care intervals with explicit start and end events
- post-fall care and rehabilitation pathways with functional, patient-reported, caregiver, and loss-to-follow-up fields
- prevention implementation and fidelity measures linked to a prespecified outcome without overclaiming causality
- source inventories and negative results showing that a proposed incidence, severity, or intervention comparison is not valid
- country- or district-bounded protocols naming the decision-maker, allocation decision, data owners, consent, safeguarding, and non-use case
- red-team review of unsafe home entry, medication changes, disability or age discrimination, false reassurance, and automated targeting

This pack does not accept:

- individual fall-risk scores, diagnosis, medication changes, exercise prescriptions, or clinical triage
- treating a fall-related mortality estimate or facility registry as population incidence without a compatible frame
- using a risk-factor association as a causal explanation or basis for withholding care
- a universal claim that one exercise, home-modification, vitamin, medication, or education package works across LMICs
- a claim that fewer reported falls proves fewer serious injuries, fractures, disability, admissions, or deaths
- automated household or neighbourhood ranking for policing, insurance, benefits, housing access, or care denial
- home visits, environmental modification, medication review, or patient-level data collection without consent, safeguarding, qualified ownership, and an exit plan

## Done Condition For V0

V0 is complete when one country- or district-bounded protocol has:

1. a reproducible older-adult fall-event denominator with age, setting, recall, case-finding, recurrence, and missingness rules
2. a separate injury and time-to-care cascade linking event, transport, facility care, transfer, rehabilitation, and functional follow-up
3. a prevention-fidelity and outcome specification that distinguishes self-report, clinical assessment, registry, and patient-defined function
4. explicit equity, consent, safeguarding, refusal, and loss-to-follow-up handling
5. an identified Ministry of Health, district, rehabilitation, housing, or funder decision that the evidence could change
6. independent replication, injury/healthy-ageing domain review, red-team review, and field-reality review before operational use

V0 is a measurement and decision-support protocol. It is not proof that a named person is likely to fall or that any intervention reduces mortality or disability outside its tested setting.
