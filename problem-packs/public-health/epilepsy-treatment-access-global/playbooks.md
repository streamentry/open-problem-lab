# Contributor and reviewer playbook

## Before contributing

Ask five questions:

1. What exactly is the unit: person, seizure episode, medicine transaction, facility-month, or population denominator?
2. What decision-maker would use the result, and on what date or planning cycle?
3. Which stage is actually observed: need, diagnosis, stock, dispensing, possession, adherence, seizure control, referral, or outcome?
4. Who is absent from the frame: people never diagnosed, people using non-government care, mobile households, children, people with stigma-related non-disclosure, or people transferred between facilities?
5. What observation would make the proposed bottleneck wrong?

## Responsible uses

- Prioritize a source audit before interpreting a treatment-gap number.
- Compare observed programme stages within one named setting when denominators and time windows are compatible.
- Identify whether a programme needs better case finding, medicine continuity, follow-up, referral linkage, or data collection before selecting an intervention.
- Design a replication or field measurement plan that protects personal health information.

## Explicit non-use cases

- Do not diagnose epilepsy from a survey, model, symptom description, or map.
- Do not recommend a medicine, dose, discontinuation, substitution, or treatment change.
- Do not infer that a facility has delivered care because a medicine appears on a national list or on a survey-day shelf.
- Do not rank countries, districts, clinicians, or facilities without domain review, equity review, and replicated denominators.
- Do not use missing records as evidence that a person is untreated.
- Do not disclose identifiable seizure histories, addresses, contacts, or medicine records.

## Reviewer prompts

### Domain reviewer

- Is the epilepsy case definition valid for the stated setting?
- Are “treatment gap,” “medicine availability,” “adherence,” and “seizure control” being used as distinct measures?
- Are clinical claims kept at the evidence grain?

### Red-team reviewer

- Could an allocation map punish facilities serving harder-to-reach patients?
- Could a procurement result be misread as a prescription or patient-level medicine decision?
- Could privacy loss or stigma be increased by linking household, clinic, and pharmacy data?
- What happens to people missing from the register?

### Field-reality reviewer

- Who owns the decision and what action is reversible?
- Does the programme have the staff, supply chain, referral pathway, and budget cycle required to act?
- What would a responsible non-use decision look like if the denominator is unknown?
