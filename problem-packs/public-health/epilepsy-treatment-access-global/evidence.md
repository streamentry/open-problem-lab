# Evidence notes

The initial records below establish why the problem is worth scoping and what a first contribution must separate. They do not constitute an accepted global treatment-gap estimate or a treatment recommendation.

## `who-epilepsy-fact-sheet-2024`

WHO's 2024 fact sheet supports global burden and treatment-gap framing, including the distinction between low-income-country treatment-gap language and the broader LMIC population. It also states that most people with epilepsy can be diagnosed and treated at primary-care level without sophisticated equipment. It cannot supply a local denominator, show that a medicine was dispensed, or infer that an individual patient will be seizure-free.

Source: [Epilepsy](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/epilepsy), accessed 2026-08-07.

## `who-epilepsy-public-health-imperative-2019`

The WHO/ILAE/IBE report is a global response and evidence frame. It is useful for identifying the intervention and governance levers a later source inventory should test: primary-care integration, provider training, medicine continuity, public information, and human-rights protections. It is not a current country-level data source and does not remove the need for local denominator and linkage review.

Source: [Epilepsy: a public health imperative](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/epilepsy-a-public-health-imperative), accessed 2026-08-07.

## `who-neurological-medicines-access-2024`

The 2024 WHO report is especially relevant to the allocation wedge. It treats epilepsy as a tracer condition for essential-medicine access and names the national, subnational, district, programme, insurance, provider, and procurement audiences. Its value here is to prevent “availability” from becoming a single undifferentiated variable. It does not supply a harmonized observed cascade for every country.

Source: [Improving access to medicines for neurological disorders](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240097377), accessed 2026-08-07.

## `cameron-antiepileptic-medicines-46-countries-2012`

This peer-reviewed WHO/HAI analysis is a primary measurement example, not a current estimate. It shows how facility surveys can separate generic availability, price ratios, and wage-based affordability across countries, and it shows why an outlet measure must not be called treatment coverage. Its age, selected medicines, facility frame, and limited phenobarbital coverage make it a baseline method reference rather than a current map.

Source: [Mapping the availability, price, and affordability of antiepileptic drugs in 46 countries](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1528-1167.2012.03446.x), accessed 2026-08-07.

## `hailemariam-asm-addis-ababa-2023`

The Addis Ababa study demonstrates a recent, local application of the WHO/HAI method. It is useful for designing a source-inventory task because it exposes outlet strata, medicine availability, price ratios, and affordability assumptions. It cannot establish national Ethiopian coverage or distinguish stock from patient possession and clinical response.

Source: [Availability, price, and affordability of antiseizure medicines in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia](https://doi.org/10.1002/epi4.12792), accessed 2026-08-07.

## `singh-home-based-epilepsy-care-2022`

The Indian cluster-randomized trial is a tractability signal. It measured a defined care model with medication use, seizure outcomes, and attrition over time. The bundled intervention, single setting, population-survey recruitment, and COVID-affected follow-up mean that it cannot be used as a global effect estimate or an automatic scale-up prescription.

Source: [A cluster-randomized trial comparing home-based primary health care and usual clinic care for epilepsy in a resource-limited country](https://doi.org/10.1002/epi4.12659), accessed 2026-08-07.

## `gbd-epilepsy-burden-2021`

The 2021 GBD analysis is the current modeled burden anchor for country and regional context. It reports active idiopathic and secondary epilepsy estimates across 204 countries and uses population-representative prevalence inputs, but it is not an observed denominator for a local cascade. Use it to define the context and candidate settings for verification, not to fill missing diagnosis, medicine, referral, or outcome stages.

Source: [Global, regional, and national burden of epilepsy, 1990-2021: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667%2824%2900302-5), accessed 2026-08-09.

## `bangladesh-national-epilepsy-survey-2017`

The WHO Bangladesh summary is a concrete population-survey source family. It documents a 2017 two-stage household sample, an urban-rural frame, response rate, prevalence, active-epilepsy classification, and a treatment-gap result. The confirmed-case denominator is small and the summary does not expose the full instrument or clinical verification, so it is usable as a setting-specific source and measurement example, not as a directly comparable global estimate.

Source: [National Prevalence of Epilepsy Survey: Summary](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/searo/bangladesh/pdf-reports/epilepsy-executive-summary.pdf?sfvrsn=64815ecd_2), accessed 2026-08-09.

## `wagner-epilepsy-care-cascade-rural-south-africa-2020`

The rural South Africa study is the strongest newly recorded care-cascade example in this inventory. It links population-based active-convulsive-epilepsy ascertainment to reported diagnosis and facility contact, then uses blood antiseizure-medicine levels to separate reported use from a biological adherence signal. Its catchment, epilepsy subtype, and incomplete stage coverage prevent national ranking or a complete referral cascade.

Source: [Epilepsy care cascade, treatment gap and its determinants in rural South Africa](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.seizure.2020.06.013), accessed 2026-08-09.

## `primary-care-epilepsy-follow-up-rural-ethiopia-2009`

The rural Ethiopia study shows why a register's “lost to follow-up” category cannot be treated as untreated. Tracing distinguished death, migration, treatment obtained elsewhere, remission, and ongoing seizures, while the study recorded a reproducible default rule. It is old and local, but it supplies a useful data model for transfer handling and missingness before any current cascade is built.

Source: [Primary care treatment of epilepsy in rural Ethiopia: Causes of default from follow-up](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.seizure.2008.07.002), accessed 2026-08-09.

## `who-igap-epilepsy-2023`

The WHO intersectoral action plan is a policy and indicator source, not an outcome dataset. Its strategic objectives and indicator annex provide a reference for mapping a local cascade to an accountable programme decision while preserving the difference between a global target and observed service coverage.

Source: [Intersectoral global action plan on epilepsy and other neurological disorders 2022–2031](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/371495/9789240076624-eng.pdf), accessed 2026-08-09.

## Evidence status

The current evidence base supports source-inventory completion and task handoff, not acceptance of a cross-country claim. The source inventory now covers modeled burden, population survey, population-based cascade, outlet medicine access, longitudinal follow-up, implementation study, and policy-indicator families. The next scoped task is `cascade-measure-dictionary`; it must find sources with enough denominator and event detail to distinguish:

1. active epilepsy from screened or clinic-attending populations;
2. diagnosis from registration;
3. medicine listing/procurement/stock from dispensing and patient possession;
4. possession and refill continuity from adherence and seizure control;
5. referral recommendation from completed referral and specialist care.
