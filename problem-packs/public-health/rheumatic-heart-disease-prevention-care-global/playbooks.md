# Playbooks

## Analyst Playbook

1. Start with `problem.json`, `evidence.json`, `datasets.md`, and `tasks.json`.
2. Pick one task and stay in its owner role.
3. Add source evidence before adding a claim or output.
4. Keep policy, procurement, stock, dispensing, administration, attendance, adherence, diagnosis, referral, surgery, and follow-up as separate events.
5. Treat hospital registries and modelled burden estimates as partial views, not complete denominators.
6. Record missingness, linkage, migration, private-sector coverage, and case-definition changes before comparing settings.

## Reviewer Playbook

Ask:

1. What exact cascade stage is being measured?
2. What is the numerator, denominator, event date, and setting?
3. Does the source observe care or only policy, stock, procurement, or referral capacity?
4. What people are invisible because they never reach the recorded system?
5. What observation would falsify the bottleneck interpretation?
6. Could a ministry or funder read this as clinical advice or as a ranking without the stated uncertainty?

## Red-Team Playbook

Stress-test for:

- labelling children or pregnant people as diseased from screening or proxy data;
- converting medicine availability or procurement into received doses or adherence;
- blaming facilities for upstream poverty, transport, supply, or referral constraints;
- excluding private, mobile, migrant, Indigenous, or otherwise marginalised populations;
- treating a hospital registry as a national denominator;
- turning an uncertain bottleneck into an automatic resource-allocation order.

## Field-Reality Playbook

Name the responsible programme user, the decision and budget cycle, the minimum data burden, the referral and appeal path, and the explicit non-use case before accepting any field-facing artifact. Ask whether the result changes a real workflow without encouraging unsafe screening, coercion, facility blame, or withdrawal of care from people missing in the data.
