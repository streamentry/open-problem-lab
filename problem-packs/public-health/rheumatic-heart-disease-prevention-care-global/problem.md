# Rheumatic Heart Disease Prevention And Valve-Care Gaps In High-Burden Countries

## Problem Statement

The missing operational object is not another global prevalence estimate. It is a verifiable account of where people are lost between prevention of group A streptococcal disease, recurrent-rheumatic-fever prophylaxis, diagnosis of rheumatic heart disease (RHD), referral, and definitive valve care.

> Which prevention-to-valve-care cascade measurements are reliable and decision-relevant enough to tell a ministry or funder whether its next marginal investment belongs in primary care, benzathine-penicillin supply and continuity, diagnosis, referral, or valve-care capacity?

This pack is deliberately narrower than “eliminate rheumatic heart disease.” It treats a burden estimate as a reason to investigate, not as a country ranking or a clinical action.

## Known Facts

- Verified fact: WHO's 24 January 2025 fact sheet estimates that RHD affects 55 million people worldwide and claims approximately 360,000 lives each year, with most of the burden in low- and middle-income countries. It identifies prevention, screening, appropriate care, reliable antibiotics, and monitoring as parts of the response. [who-rhd-fact-sheet-2025]
- Verified fact: WHO's 2024 guideline covers primary prevention of rheumatic fever, secondary prevention through long-term antibiotic prophylaxis and adherence support, screening for early RHD, and management of rheumatic fever. A guideline is not evidence that a country delivers any of those stages. [who-rhd-guideline-2024]
- Verified fact: World Health Assembly resolution WHA71.14 asks Member States to estimate burden, strengthen surveillance and data collection, improve primary-care access, ensure reliable access to diagnostic and treatment tools, and monitor prevention and control efforts. [wha71-14-rheumatic-heart-disease-2018]
- Verified fact: A 2019 analysis using G-FINDER and Global Burden of Disease data reported approximately US$1.2 million in RHD research funding in 2017 and the highest disability-neglect index among the 16 tropical diseases it assessed. This is historical product-related R&D evidence, not a current estimate of all implementation or service-delivery spending. [macleod-rhd-underfunding-2019]
- Verified fact: The REMEDY registry followed 3,343 people with clinical RHD in 14 low- and middle-income countries; among participants with known 24-month status, 500 deaths were recorded. The cohort demonstrates severe outcomes among referred patients, not population mortality or national coverage. [remedy-rhd-registry-2016]
- Verified fact: A 2025 mixed-methods study in two Northern Uganda districts evaluated decentralising benzathine-penicillin prophylaxis from hospitals to eight primary-health centres among 151 enrolled patients and found non-inferior adherence in that setting. It is a tractability signal, not a general effectiveness estimate. [minja-uganda-rhd-decentralisation-2025]

## Decision Wedge

The named decision-makers are a ministry of health's NCD, child-health, or primary-care programme managers and a funder deciding what to finance in a named high-burden setting. The evidence that would change the decision is a reviewed and replicated cascade table showing, with explicit denominators and missingness, whether the binding measurable gap is:

1. prevention and early treatment of suspected streptococcal infection;
2. initiation and continuity of secondary prophylaxis;
3. early diagnosis and case registration;
4. referral and continuity across levels of care; or
5. access to definitive valve care and documented follow-up.

The pack does not assume that the largest observed gap is the most cost-effective intervention. It creates the measurement needed to test that assumption.

## Uncertain Areas

- Whether any country can link primary-care, registry, laboratory, pharmacy, referral, and surgical records without double-counting or exposing identifiable patient data.
- How often procurement, stock-on-hand, dispensing, injection administration, attendance, and prophylaxis adherence are being conflated in reported programme coverage.
- Whether echocardiographic screening results can be interpreted consistently across devices, readers, age groups, and case definitions.
- How much hospital-based registries overrepresent severe disease and underrepresent people who never reach specialist care.
- Whether a cascade denominator can include people receiving care outside the public system, people who move, and people lost after pregnancy or school-based screening.
- Which parts of the Ugandan decentralisation result, if any, transfer to other settings with different workforce, medicine-supply, transport, and referral conditions.

## Initial Scope

This pack accepts:

- Dated inventories of RHD burden, registry, primary-care, medicine-supply, screening, referral, and valve-care data sources.
- A measure dictionary that separates policy, procurement, stock, dispensing, administration, attendance, adherence, diagnosis, referral, surgery, and follow-up.
- De-identified or synthetic linkage and missingness audits.
- One-country or one-network baseline analyses that preserve event grain, timing, and uncertainty.
- Red-team and field-reality reviews of clinical overreach, inequity, privacy, stigma, and resource-allocation misuse.

This pack does not accept:

- Individual diagnosis, screening eligibility, antibiotic, anticoagulant, surgery, or other clinical advice.
- Country rankings based only on modeled prevalence, mortality, or a hospital registry.
- Claims that medicine procurement, stock availability, or a written policy equals a received dose, adherence, diagnosis, or valve-care outcome.
- Extrapolation of a single-site intervention result into a global recommendation without independent replication.
- Identifiable patient, child, pregnancy, school, or facility-level records.

## V0 Done Condition

The pack is useful when one named country or care network has a reviewed and independently replicated cascade artifact that distinguishes at least prevention, prophylaxis, diagnosis, referral, and definitive-care stages, reports missingness and selection boundaries, and names a responsible programme decision without issuing clinical instructions.
